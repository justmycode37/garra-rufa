## Research gaps

A mechanism established in the disease and an intervention acting on it that the literature has not (or has) tested in the disease.

- **neuronal Ighmbp2 cDNA expression** → motor neuron — untested, plausibility 3/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: neuronal Ighmbp2 cDNA expression rescues neuromuscular degeneration
  - rationale: Neuronal IGHMBP2 replacement directly targets a genetically established motor-neuron degeneration mechanism in IGHMBP2-related SMA and has supporting rescue evidence in a motor-neuron degeneration model.
  - caveat: The rescue was shown in nmd mice rather than an SMN1-deficient SMA model, and AAV delivery, expression timing, transduction efficiency, and dose remain major translational considerations.
  - experiment: First test neuronal IGHMBP2 cDNA delivery in an IGHMBP2-deficient or severe SMA animal model, measuring motor-neuron survival, neuromuscular function, and survival benefit.
  - evidence: "transgenic expression of the Ighmbp2 cDNA prevented primary motor neuron degeneration, while restoring the normal axonal morphology and density in nmd mice." (PMID:15069027, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "SMN1, which encodes the survival motor neuron (SMN) protein essential for the development and viability of motor neurons in the ventral spinal cord" (PMID:35715566, P4)
- **CK-2017357** → skeletal muscle contractility — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: CK-2017357 tested_in myasthenia gravis
  - rationale: A fast skeletal-muscle troponin sensitizer could transiently improve force generation in SMA patients whose weakness is partly caused by surviving motor neurons being unable to activate muscle effectively.
  - caveat: The cited evidence is from a myasthenia-gravis model and does not establish benefit in denervated or atrophic SMA muscle, while systemic effects and long-term safety require evaluation.
  - experiment: First conduct a muscle-biopsy or isolated-muscle study using SMA patient-derived fibers to determine whether CK-2017357 increases contractile force at physiologic activation levels.
  - evidence: "sensitization of the fast skeletal troponin complex to calcium improves muscle force and grip strength immediately after single doses of CK-2017357 in a model of neuromuscular disease, myasthenia gravis" (PMID:22344294, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "CK-2127107 helps increase skeletal muscle contractility and enhances physical performance in patients with neuromuscular diseases." (PMID:27490705, P39)
- **caffeine** → motor neuron degeneration — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: caffeine rescues motor neuron degeneration
  - rationale: Caffeine is a readily available systemic compound with a plausible neuroprotective or symptomatic-testing rationale in a motor-neuron disease, and the supplied evidence reports reduced motor-neuron loss in mice.
  - caveat: The evidence does not specify the model or establish preservation of SMN function or meaningful clinical benefit in SMA, and adverse cardiovascular and stimulant effects may limit dosing.
  - experiment: First test caffeine in a severe SMN-deficient mouse model with blinded motor-neuron counts, neuromuscular-function measurements, survival, and tolerability monitoring.
  - evidence: "Histological analyses revealed that significantly fewer motor neurons were lost in caffeine-treated mice at p41, despite no changes in soma morphology." (PMID:40931339, P4)
  - mechanism evidence: "Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) is an autosomal recessive neuromuscular disorder of motor neurons that manifests with weakness caused by widespread skeletal muscle denervation atrophy." (PMID:38330285, P8)
