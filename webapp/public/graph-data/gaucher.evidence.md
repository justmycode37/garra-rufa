## Research gaps

A mechanism established in the disease and an intervention acting on it that the literature has not (or has) tested in the disease.

- **bicyclic pyrazole urea GCS inhibitor** → glucosylceramide synthase — untested, plausibility 3/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: bicyclic pyrazole urea GCS inhibitor targets glucosylceramide synthase
  - rationale: A CNS-penetrant glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor directly reduces the upstream production of glucosylceramide, a central pathogenic substrate in Gaucher disease.
  - caveat: Systemic GCS inhibition may cause substantial glycosphingolipid disturbances, and benefit may depend on Gaucher subtype, tissue distribution, and achieving useful exposure in the brain and viscera.
  - experiment: Evaluate the bicyclic pyrazole urea inhibitor in GBA1-deficient neuronal and macrophage cultures, followed by dose-ranging studies in a neuronopathic Gaucher mouse model measuring substrate clearance, functional outcomes, and safety.
  - evidence: "we report the optimization of a bicyclic pyrazole amide GCS inhibitor discovered through HTS to low dose, oral, CNS penetrant, bicyclic pyrazole urea GCSi's with in vivo activity in mouse models" (PMID:36793422, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "The reduction of brain glycosphingolipids as a consequence of systemic drug administration strongly suggests that GZ 161 is both capable of crossing the blood brain barrier and inhibiting its target enzyme, GCS." (PMID:22912851, P10)
- **benzoxazole 1** → GBA1 — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: benzoxazole 1 tested_in Parkinson's disease
  - rationale: Reducing glycosphingolipids through a brain-active compound could plausibly address the central substrate accumulation in Gaucher disease, particularly neuronopathic forms.
  - caveat: The quoted evidence does not establish the precise molecular target, Gaucher efficacy, safety, or whether the compound acts directly on GBA1 or GCS in patients.
  - experiment: Test BZ1 in GBA1-deficient neuronal cells and Gaucher mouse models for restoration of lysosomal glucocerebroside flux, GCase activity, and neuronal function while measuring systemic exposure and toxicity.
  - evidence: "In vivo studies revealed BZ1 had pharmacological activity and reduced glycosphingolipids in the mouse brain to a similar extent observed in neuronal cultures." (PMID:34509608, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "Gaucher disease (GD) is an autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder that results from loss of function of acid β-glucosidase" (PMID:22976765, P7)
- **(3R,4R,5S)-5-octylpiperidine-3,4-diol** → acid β-glucocerebrosidase — untested, plausibility 1/3, evidence in_vitro, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: (3R,4R,5S)-5-octylpiperidine-3,4-diol targets acid β-glucocerebrosidase
  - rationale: Although the compound is pharmacologically active at acid β-glucocerebrosidase, inhibiting an enzyme already deficient in Gaucher disease is mechanistically counterproductive.
  - caveat: The quoted enzyme-inhibition result creates a strong risk of worsening glucocerebroside accumulation unless the compound can instead function as an effective chaperone or stabilizer.
  - experiment: First determine whether it restores or preserves GCase activity in GBA1-mutant cells at non-inhibitory, chaperone-like concentrations; discontinue development if direct inhibition predominates.
  - evidence: "An IC50 of 11 nM was notably found for the most active C-Oct derivative 6b, barely superior to that of O-Hept DIX 8." (PMID:30669468, P15)
  - mechanism evidence: "genetic alterations leading to deficiency in lysosomal GlcCer degradation by GBA cause Gaucher disease (GD)" (PMID:30669468, P4)
- **macrophage-targeted human placental glucocerebrosidase** → β-glucocerebrosidase activity — barely_tested, plausibility 0/3, evidence clinical_trial, 1 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: macrophage-targeted human placental glucocerebrosidase targets β-glucocerebrosidase activity
  - rationale: Macrophage-targeted glucocerebrosidase directly replaces the deficient lysosomal enzyme, making the rationale central to Gaucher disease, but this is an established enzyme-replacement approach rather than an untested gap.
  - caveat: The historical placental-derived preparation is not a novel modern therapy and would require comparison with approved recombinant enzyme formulations.
  - experiment: No new efficacy trial is warranted; instead, compare macrophage targeting and biodistribution against approved imiglucerase and taliglucerase alfa in preclinical models.
  - evidence: "we infused macrophage-targeted human placental glucocerebrosidase (60 IU per kilogram of body weight every 2 weeks for 9 to 12 months)" (PMID:2023606, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "Type 1 Gaucher disease is an inherited lysosomal disorder caused by impaired activity of β-glucocerebrosidase" (PMID:23270487, P7)
- **small-molecule chaperones** → acid β-glucosidase — tested, evidence in_vitro, 12 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
