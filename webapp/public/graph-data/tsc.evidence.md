- **Tsc2 mRNA–loaded hybrid lipid nanoparticle** → TSC2 — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: Tsc2 mRNA–loaded hybrid lipid nanoparticle targets TSC2
  - rationale: Delivering functional TSC2 mRNA directly addresses the causal loss-of-function mechanism and is especially compelling for TSC2-deficient patients.
  - caveat: The approach is genotype-specific, and systemic delivery to relevant tissues, expression durability, immunogenicity, oncogenic effects, and possible competition with mTOR-inhibitor therapy remain unresolved.
  - experiment: Run a dose-ranging Tsc2-deficient animal study measuring TSC2 protein restoration and rescue of kidney and CNS lesions while monitoring mTOR-pathway activation and toxicity.
  - evidence: "We next evaluated the therapeutic effect of hLNPs loaded with Tsc2 mRNA in this preclinical model." (PMID:35173043, P23)
  - mechanism evidence: "The disease causing genes (TSC1 and TSC2) have been identified and the “two-hit” mechanism of disease has been demonstrated in many tumors" (PMID:21915260, P7)
- **fenobam** → GRM5 — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence clinical_trial, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: fenobam targets GRM5
  - rationale: Fenobam directly inhibits the mGluR5 signaling implicated in abnormal hippocampal plasticity in a Tsc2 mouse model, and prior clinical exposure provides a useful translational starting point.
  - caveat: The evidence establishes elevated receptor expression and involvement in one plasticity phenotype rather than proving that mGluR5 drives seizures or developmental disability, and developmental psychiatric safety requires careful assessment.
  - experiment: Test fenobam in Tsc2 heterozygous mice with blinded measurement of seizures, cortical excitability, learning, and autistic-like behaviors after establishing target engagement.

- **PMID:18809716** TSC-mTOR maintains quiescence and function of hematopoietic stem cells by repressing mitochondrial biogenesis and reactive oxygen species. (J Exp Med, 2008, cited by 576, same disease, relevance 3/3)
  Conditional Tsc1 deletion in adult mouse HSCs activated mTOR, drove loss of quiescence, increased mitochondrial biogenesis and reactive oxygen species, and impaired long-term hematopoietic stem-cell function.
