  - edge: ML-SA5 rescues globotriaosylceramide
  - rationale: ML-SA5 reduced Gb3 accumulation in the cited experimental system, directly aligning with the pathogenic storage lipid targeted by Fabry therapies.
  - caveat: The quote does not identify the disease model, cellular mechanism, or selectivity of ML-SA5, and there is no evidence yet of activity in GLA-deficient human cells or relevant tissues.
  - experiment: Establish ML-SA5 dose-response curves in GLA-deficient Fabry fibroblasts and cardiomyocytes, quantifying intracellular Gb3 and lyso-Gb3 alongside viability, lysosomal pH, and GLA activity.
  - evidence: "Gb3 accumulation, visualized using Cy3-labeled Shiga toxin (ShTx-B-Cy3), was also decreased in a ML-SA5 concentration-dependent manner" (PMID:39080379, P20)
  - mechanism evidence: "associated accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) and globotriaosylsphingosine (lyso-Gb3)." (PMID:37940383, P8)
- **tamoxifen** → globotriaosylceramide — barely_tested, plausibility 2/3, evidence in_vitro, 1 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: tamoxifen tested_in CLN3 disease
  - rationale: Tamoxifen promoted clearance of lysosomal Gb3 in CLN3 patient fibroblasts, making it a mechanistically relevant candidate for reducing the central Gb3 burden in Fabry disease.
  - caveat: The evidence is indirect, limited to one other lysosomal storage disease at 10 µM, and tamoxifen has clinically important adverse effects that may outweigh benefit in a slowly progressive disease.
  - experiment: Test tamoxifen in GLA-deficient Fabry patient fibroblasts and induced pluripotent stem-cell-derived cardiomyocytes using dose- and time-dependent Gb3 and lyso-Gb3 reduction while measuring viability and lysosomal function.
  - evidence: "We confirmed that 10µM tamoxifen promotes the clearance of lysosomal Gb3, stained with STX, in human CLN3 patient fibroblasts" (PMID:34411438, P16)
  - mechanism evidence: "associated accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) and globotriaosylsphingosine (lyso-Gb3)." (PMID:37940383, P8)
- **trehalose** → lysosomal storage — untested, plausibility 1/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: trehalose rescues lysosomal storage
  - rationale: Trehalose reduced autophagic vacuoles in an animal model, offering a possible way to improve lysosomal clearance in Fabry disease.
  - caveat: The evidence concerns a nonspecific vacuolar phenotype rather than demonstrated restoration of GLA activity or clearance of Gb3, and systemic trehalose efficacy and tolerability remain uncertain.
  - experiment: Evaluate trehalose in GLA-deficient fibroblasts and a Fabry animal model by measuring Gb3 burden, autophagic flux, renal and cardiac function, and safety.
  - evidence: "Trehalose treatment significantly reduced the number of large autophagic vacuoles" (PMID:29916295, P27)
  - mechanism evidence: "FD (McKusick 301500) is an X-linked multisystemic lysosomal storage disorder" (PMID:25885911, P9)

  - rationale: Human amniotic epithelial cells provide a plausible cross-correction platform if transplanted cells could take up and degrade circulating Gb3.

  - edge: HP-β-cyclodextrin tested_in Niemann-Pick disease type C
