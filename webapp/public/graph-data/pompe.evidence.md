## Research gaps

A mechanism established in the disease and an intervention acting on it that the literature has not (or has) tested in the disease.

- **supervised aerobic and progressive resistance exercise training** → GAA — barely_tested, plausibility 3/3, evidence review, 1 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: supervised aerobic and progressive resistance exercise training treats glycogen storage disease due to acid maltase deficiency, late-onset
  - rationale: Supervised aerobic and resistance training directly addresses exercise intolerance and muscle weakness in acid-maltase-deficiency myopathy and is supportive care with a favorable safety profile when individualized.
  - caveat: Training must be adapted to cardiac involvement, respiratory status, and risk of rhabdomyolysis, and it should complement rather than replace GAA-directed disease-modifying therapy.
  - experiment: Conduct a randomized, assessor-blinded trial comparing individualized supervised training with usual care in stable patients, using endurance, strength, respiratory function, and adverse-event endpoints.
  - evidence: "Exercise training resulted in a significant increase in muscular strength and 6-min walking distance despite no change in total and lower extremity lean body mass" (PMID:26471939, P87)
  - mechanism evidence: "Pompe disease (PD; OMIM 232300) is a severe metabolic myopathy caused by the deficiency of acid alpha-glucosidase" (PMID:23606558, P4)
- **AAVrh74.tMCK.hBAG3 gene therapy** → autophagy — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: AAVrh74.tMCK.hBAG3 gene therapy targets autophagy
  - rationale: Muscle-directed BAG3 expression is a plausible way to improve chaperone-mediated disposal of damaged autophagic components implicated in Pompe myopathy.
  - caveat: The evidence provided is conceptual rather than functional, and AAV delivery, pre-existing immunity, expression durability, and whether BAG3 affects beneficial as well as defective autophagy remain unresolved.
  - experiment: Administer AAVrh74.tMCK.hBAG3 to GAA-deficient mice and measure BAG3 expression, autophagic flux, lysosomal glycogen, and muscle function.
  - evidence: "Utilizing BAG3 gene therapy is based on the premise that pathophysiological aspects of these common pathways are pertinent to the development of treatment strategies." (PMID:42137292, P25)
  - mechanism evidence: "LC3, encoded by the mammalian homolog of the yeast atg8 gene, and its processed phospholipid-conjugated LC3-II molecular form, localized in the autophagosomes, are known markers of autophagy" (PMID:19046416, P16)
- **afucosylated rhesusized anti-CD20** → anti-drug antibody response — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: afucosylated rhesusized anti-CD20 rescues anti-drug antibody response
  - rationale: B-cell depletion could reduce anti-drug antibodies that neutralize or clear Pompe enzyme replacement therapy, providing a clear strategy for immunogenicity management.
  - caveat: Anti-CD20 is not antigen-specific, can produce serious infection risks, and may fail to eliminate established memory B-cell responses or tolerance to GAA.
  - experiment: Pilot anti-CD20 pretreatment in GAA-deficient mice receiving recombinant human GAA and measure ADA development, GAA uptake, glycogen clearance, and immune safety.
  - evidence: "By contrast, the 5 animals of this study which underwent B cell depletion prior to VRC01v23 infusion received three 10 mg/kg doses of the bNAb with minimal development of ADA endpoint titers" (PMID:36157588, P15)
  - mechanism evidence: "Notably, high sustained antibodies, which compromise the clinical efficacy of ERT, can indeed develop in CRIM-positive patients receiving rhGAA." (PMID:38715621, P37)
- **treadmill training** → autophagy — barely_tested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 1 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: treadmill training rescues autophagy
  - rationale: Treadmill training could improve muscle conditioning and may normalize autophagic flux, making it a plausible adjunct to disease-modifying therapy in Pompe disease.
  - caveat: Unsupervised or excessive exercise may cause rhabdomyolysis in GAA-deficient muscle, and restored autophagic flux in another model does not establish benefit in Pompe disease.
  - experiment: Run a controlled treadmill study in GAA-deficient mice with graded intensity, measuring autophagic flux, muscle injury biomarkers, glycogen, and functional performance.
  - evidence: "restored the suppressed autophagic flux" (PMID:38185293, P4)
  - mechanism evidence: "LC3, encoded by the mammalian homolog of the yeast atg8 gene, and its processed phospholipid-conjugated LC3-II molecular form, localized in the autophagosomes, are known markers of autophagy" (PMID:19046416, P16)
- **[E1-, E2b-]adenovirus vector encoding human acid alpha-glucosidase** → lysosomal glycogen accumulation — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: [E1-, E2b-]adenovirus vector encoding human acid alpha-glucosidase rescues lysosomal glycogen accumulation
  - rationale: Direct skeletal-muscle delivery of human GAA addresses the enzyme deficiency and glycogen-filled lysosomes that are central to Pompe disease.
  - caveat: Early-generation adenoviral vectors have substantial immunogenicity, limited durability, and potentially hepatotropic or inflammatory effects, making them less attractive than contemporary gene-therapy platforms.
  - experiment: Evaluate dose-dependent vector delivery in GAA-deficient muscle tissue, measuring transduction, long-term GAA expression, glycogen clearance, anti-vector immunity, and functional outcomes.
  - evidence: "Importantly, once localized to skeletal muscle lysosomes, the hGAA was able to effectively clear the glycogen accumulations present in AMD quail muscles." (PMID:12731088, P4)
  - mechanism evidence: "With a deficiency of this enzyme, as seen in Pompe disease, there is abnormal accumulation of glycogen and progressive expansion of these glycogen-filled lysosomes." (PMID:36969713, P10)
