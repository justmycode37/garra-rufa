- **bortezomib-based immune modulation regimen** → anti-drug antibody response — untested, plausibility 3/3, evidence review, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: bortezomib-based immune modulation regimen rescues anti-drug antibody response
  - rationale: Bortezomib directly addresses pathogenic anti-drug antibodies, a major cause of enzyme-replacement failure, and the quoted review explicitly reports successful use in Pompe disease.
  - caveat: Despite the claimed absence of naming papers or trials, the quote itself describes Pompe-specific use, and bortezomib carries substantial neuropathy, infection, and hematologic toxicity.
  - experiment: Evaluate bortezomib-based immunomodulation in anti-GAA-antibody-positive Pompe patients with paired antibody titers, urinary GAA levels, enzyme uptake, and carefully monitored safety endpoints.
  - evidence: "Immunomodulation therapy has also been successful for patients who develop high ADA titers during ERT for Pompe disease using a regimen based on bortezomib" (PMID:28119821, P23)
