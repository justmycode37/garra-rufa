## Research gaps

A mechanism established in the disease and an intervention acting on it that the literature has not (or has) tested in the disease.

- **regular treadmill exercise** → synaptic plasticity — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: regular treadmill exercise rescues synaptic plasticity
  - rationale: Reduced synaptic plasticity is a plausible contributor to the neurodevelopmental and ataxic phenotype of Angelman syndrome, and supervised treadmill exercise is a low-risk way to test whether activity-dependent plasticity can be enhanced.
  - caveat: The supporting evidence is preclinical and indirect, while Angelman syndrome-related ataxia, obesity, and behavioral needs may limit treadmill participation and generalizability.
  - experiment: Run a randomized, assessor-blinded, supervised aerobic treadmill program in children with Angelman syndrome, comparing synaptic-plasticity biomarkers and exploratory gait, cognition, and quality-of-life outcomes with an active control.
  - evidence: "exercise significantly activated neuronal autophagy in the hippocampal CA1 region via the Wnt5a/CamkII signaling pathway, which enhanced synaptic plasticity" (PMID:39389946, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "We found a full recovery of the synaptic plasticity defect normally observed in the AS mouse model (Figure 1) [20]." (PMID:25491305, P13)
