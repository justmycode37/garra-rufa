## Research gaps

- **momelotinib** → ACVR1 — untested, plausibility 3/3, evidence database, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: momelotinib targets ACVR1
  - rationale: Momelotinib inhibits ALK2, the constitutively active ACVR1 receptor that drives the central ossification pathway in FOP, and it is an approved drug with systemic clinical use.
  - caveat: Its lack of ALK2 selectivity means JAK1/2 inhibition could confound efficacy and introduce hematologic and infectious risks.
  - experiment: Test momelotinib in patient-derived FOP mesenchymal progenitor cells and ACVR1-mutant mice for inhibition of SMAD1/5/8 signaling, chondrogenesis, and heterotopic ossification.
  - evidence: "Momelotinib (Figure 11A), a JAK1/2 inhibitor, is equipotent at ALK2 and moderately selective over ALK subtypes." (PMID:34423181, P35)
  - mechanism evidence: "FOP is caused by heterozygous missense mutations in ACVR1, the gene encoding activin A receptor type 1, a bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) type I receptor13." (PMID:37770652, P6)
- **Inhba small interfering RNA** → INHBA — untested, plausibility 3/3, evidence in_vitro, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: Inhba small interfering RNA targets INHBA
  - rationale: Direct Inhba knockdown closely matches evidence that AAV-mediated Inhba silencing in ACVR1-mutant cells reduces a relevant activin pathway, and siRNA provides a concrete therapeutic modality.
  - caveat: INHBA is not the causal ACVR1 mutation, and effective, safe, tissue-selective delivery while preserving necessary activin functions remains a major challenge.
  - experiment: Deliver optimized Inhba siRNA to ACVR1-mutant FOP progenitor cells and Acvr1R206H mice, measuring INHBA knockdown, SMAD signaling, and heterotopic bone volume.
