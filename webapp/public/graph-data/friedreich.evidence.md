## Research gaps

A mechanism established in the disease and an intervention acting on it that the literature has not (or has) tested in the disease.

- **myricetin** → mitochondrial dysfunction — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence in_vitro, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: myricetin rescues mitochondrial dysfunction
  - rationale: Myricetin improved mitochondrial morphology in an in-vitro setting, making it a reasonable exploratory intervention for the mitochondrial dysfunction associated with FRDA.
  - caveat: The quoted cellular evidence does not establish that myricetin activates Nrf2 or rescues FXN deficiency specifically, and its pharmacology and safety in FRDA require clarification.
  - experiment: Treat FRDA patient-derived lymphoblasts or induced pluripotent stem-cell-derived neurons and cardiomyocytes with myricetin and measure mitochondrial membrane potential, morphology, oxidative stress, and function.
  - evidence: "However, myricetin treatment gradually ameliorated mitochondrial morphology, with the electron density of mitochondrial matrix and the number of cristae being restored." (PMID:41317582, P24)
  - mechanism evidence: "in Friedreich ataxia patients, as well as in yeast and mouse models, this is associated with mitochondrial dysfunction" (PMID:25499576, P10)
- **MitoTEMPO** → oxidative stress — untested, plausibility 2/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: MitoTEMPO targets oxidative stress
  - rationale: MitoTEMPO directly targets the oxidative-stress component associated with mitochondrial iron accumulation in Friedreich ataxia and could provide a plausible antioxidant strategy.
  - caveat: The supporting evidence is from placental inflammation rather than FRDA, and systemic mitochondrial-targeted antioxidant effects and long-term safety are uncertain.
  - experiment: Administer MitoTEMPO to an FRDA animal model and measure mitochondrial ROS, iron handling, mitochondrial function, cardiac injury, and neurological deterioration.
  - evidence: "In the lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced PTB mouse model, MT inhibited PTB by ameliorating maternal oxidative stress and inflammation, especially in placentas" (PMID:38573008, P2)
  - mechanism evidence: "mitochondrial iron accumulation, cytosolic iron depletion, and increased oxidative stress" (PMID:25499576, P10)
- **quercetin** → FXN — untested, plausibility 1/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: quercetin rescues FXN
  - rationale: Quercetin restores reduced frataxin in oleate/palmitate-stressed liver or HepG2 cells, but this does not directly address FXN loss, the causal defect in Friedreich ataxia.
  - caveat: The intervention evidence concerns acquired frataxin depletion rather than FRDA, and it is unclear whether quercetin can safely restore frataxin in disease-relevant tissues.
  - experiment: Test quercetin in FRDA patient-derived fibroblasts and FXN-deficient neuronal or cardiomyocyte models using frataxin protein, mitochondrial function, and viability endpoints.
  - evidence: "frataxin was lowered in the liver of HFD-fed mice or HepG2 cell incubated with oleate/palmitate but restored by quercetin" (PMID:29935106, P3)
  - mechanism evidence: "Frataxin levels were measured in buccal cells and blood by lateral flow assay." (PMID:30911578, P9)
- **Herba houttuyniae aqueous extract** → oxidative stress — untested, plausibility 1/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: Herba houttuyniae aqueous extract rescues oxidative stress
  - rationale: Oxidative stress is biologically important in Friedreich ataxia, but the quoted effect of Herba houttuyniae is limited to suppressing protein carbonylation in non-neurological disease models.
  - caveat: A heterogeneous aqueous extract with liver and heart effects in another animal model has uncertain composition, tissue exposure, and neurological benefit.
  - experiment: Evaluate a standardized Houttuynia cordata extract in an FRDA animal model with mitochondrial ROS, frataxin levels, neurological function, and cardiac outcomes.
  - evidence: "HAE supplementation effectively inhibited the elevation of protein carbonyl levels in both the liver and heart" (PMID:31936037, P25)
  - mechanism evidence: "mitochondrial iron accumulation, cytosolic iron depletion, and increased oxidative stress" (PMID:25499576, P10)
- **CAPNS1 restoration** → mitochondrial dysfunction — untested, plausibility 1/3, evidence animal, 0 papers on it in the disease, 0 trials
  - edge: CAPNS1 restoration tested_in Alzheimer's disease (AD)
  - rationale: CAPNS1 restoration improved mitochondrial cristae integrity in an Alzheimer disease mouse model, but its relationship to the FXN-loss mechanisms of Friedreich ataxia is indirect.
  - caveat: CAPNS1 upregulation is a disease-model-specific gene manipulation rather than an established therapeutic intervention, with possible calpain-related effects and no evidence of FRDA efficacy.
  - experiment: Use CAPNS1 modulation in FRDA patient-derived cells and an FRDA animal model to assess mitochondrial structure, frataxin stability, oxidative stress, and neurological phenotypes.
